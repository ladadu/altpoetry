DUMPSTER[101.mo]

A bright June day, summer. Neighbors from the house opposite, rhythmically, step by step, are throwing away trash. They pass by my window every 10–20 minutes—some with a box, some with a large bag. They approach a huge metal green dumpster and overturn the contents of the box, bag, or package into it.p>

Just a week ago, Edna - thin elderly woman with a deep and somewhat guarded gaze lived in that house. She rarely went out into the street and seldom conversed with anyone; she would stand, watch, and smile silently when our eyes met. She died, having completed her life cycle. Now, her relatives were hurriedly getting rid of all the things they had been forced to endure along with their owner.

They were clearing the space. Furniture, rugs, curtains were not being thrown away—they were throwing away the "trifle," those capsules of memory that could only be extracted by the one who was no longer there. Here, a young woman drags another cardboard box to the dumpster, filled to the top with something that shimmers with a white glaze in the sun. That is not how Chinese plastic shines. The box is overturned, and pieces of daily life and memories fall like a granular waterfall into the fractured emptiness, making one last ringing sound of their integrity. This is bisque—that is how fine porcelain sounds. The woman with an air of a well-performed job steps aside for a new batch of submissive trash.

My gaze can no longer remain locked behind the window glass—I exit, approaching this cemetery of fine sound, and look into the green metallic abyss... Yes, not a single piece of plastic, but a multitude of histories. Histories not in books, not in letters and paragraphs. Histories in process, in the distance between steps, in the direction of these strides, and at the point of origin..

From the metallic throat, I pull out a small porcelain vase with the most delicate relief of frozen flowers and weaves. Translucent chipped petals, lines of fracture look fresh and, it seems, still call out for that broken-off piece, refusing to believe in their own disfigurement. The drawing, line, color, transition are broken, interrupted. And here is a tiny porcelain figurine of a girl in a red beret with a basket of fruit. She survived almost completely intact—just a chip on the base.

I hold this mini-world in my hands, and it unlocks its voice for me, allowing me to see... I see the intense gaze of the artist nervously erasing yet another "imperfect" line. The unhurried movements of the studio master, peering into his workers and asking into the void: who among them will be able to become his successor-follower, one who knows how to see, feel, ask questions, search... I see tears in the eyes of the sculptor who cannot come to terms with the terrible illness of his friend. He is poor and ready to mold anything, anytime, anywhere, without rest or sleep. I see a young woman, and together with her brush, I apply red paint onto the white porcelain. Her thoughts are both here and there—at home, where her kids have come from school, where her husband, mother, sister are... A smile of tenderness and light anxiety is frozen upon her face...

What drove these people? Love, the desire to create, show, reveal, and search, and also—to pass on everything they discovered themselves, like a frame, like a lighthouse for those who find themselves in an infinite ocean of cognition. Every final stroke on a sketch, a found line of form, a composition of colors, taken in hand by a brush, stroke, and a thin line drawn under tension—this is a trace. The trace of those who waited for mother to return from work while she applied paint; the trace of the artist’s thoughts as he recalled his childhood and that girl who slyly smiled at him. These are dreams of opening a studio of arts and giving away to all who came the found pearls of mastery and infinite lines of beauty. This is the sleepless night and worry of the sculptor, fearing there will not be enough earned money for the treatment of a friend..

Money... They are not the last element in this capsule. Without them, this porcelain girl would never have stood on a wooden chest of drawers and summoned a wave of warmth in the heart of another girl, who has already passed her 80-year path. There was an entrepreneur, there was an idea, there were opportunities, the conveyor was launched, people were hired, and their histories, steps, and desires began to "make money." The money went to the owner, the masters received theirs, and the porcelain girl — recived an opportunity to be.

But something went wrong. Additional material values began to appear on the scales opposite the money. Compared to them, life histories, the memory of roads, tears, and smiles were ephemeral, insignificant. They could be ignored while moving toward the goal. Faster, cheaper, mass-produced... And now plastic smiling figurines with printed faces in plastic boxes wait for the outstretched hands of children. What will the children see in them, what kind of history will they read? Will this be a history of a thin pencil lead, or a history of copyright agreements and stock markets?..

I carry the porcelain girl in the red beret and the vase with chipped petals home; they will now be a memory capsule of my memory, which no one can open except me... Within it, the image of this June day will live.

— Katya 101.mo
