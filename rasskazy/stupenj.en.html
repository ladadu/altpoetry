The Step [101.mo]

RU

More than 15 years have passed, the knot has loosened, and she got glasses, scissors, and a untangling hook...

If HELL exists, that was it, its deepest circle.
She reached the precipice, the abyss, and was already hanging on its edge, clinging to the remnants of logical hope with the fingers of her will, but gravity was stronger. She tried to feel for a step in the abyss, but it wasn't there then. Around her were people, relatives, good, kind, loving people, but they didn't see her abyss, they saw a young, smart, beautiful woman who now has a infant daughter, a caring husband, a material base, health, and peacetime. She gave these people signs. Each of them, being in the cocoon of their own story, feelings, and beliefs, reacted based on them...

Husband.
They are walking to a store near the house, summer, sun. He shines with joy from the realization that she is nearby. He is tired from night shifts at work, but that is nothing—light clouds on the bright horizon NOW.
She walks, listening to her inner self, from time to time raises her head, looks at the figures of people, wonders—why do they all want to acquire, tell, show something... or are they, like her, automatically doing what is customary to do, while inside shuddering from the screaming meaninglessness.
She looks at her husband, slows her pace, stops. She looks into his blue eyes, in which there is still so much of childhood, she tries with sound, with meaning to penetrate to where she will be heard. It seems to anyone that she is screaming with her quiet submission:
—You know—I feel bad, very bad..."
The husband stops, thinks, it seems to her—he caught on. A few more seconds of his deepening into thought, and:
—No... Because I feel good!
They walk on... She has no more reason to tell him about her abyss.

Father.
Father came to visit. He doesn't go to visit people, only her, and even then, only on business and not for long.
They are alike, he is her source. She trusts him.
This time he came to her to get a haircut. Father has a huge bald spot down to the back of his head, which he covers with the remnants of hair growing on the sides. Nobody wears it like that, but he does, and that is—law. How she loves this bald spot of his and these rare strands of hair on it. But a haircut is a ritual, even if there is almost nothing to cut. He sits on a chair, wrapped in a plastic cape. She hovers around him, changing now scissors, now a comb, now a hair clipper in her hands. The process is serious, almost to the point of solemnity. And silence... She can almost not hold back tears from the multiple torments. He is—so close, he will understand now, and if he doesn't give advice, he will at least let her know that he understood.
— Papa, it's very hard for me, I really feel bad...
— Why, you have everything good—look... You have everything...
And then quietly:
— I understand, little girl. You have to endure...
Endure..She endures, but she can't anymore. Papa... How much do you endure? And is this what you wish for me too?

Mother.
There is this bouncy ball—rubber, heavy, dense, it ricochets off everything without changing its own essence, but it can destroy everything fragile around it. And it is this very bouncy ball, mother, that has endless energy and an off-center gravity. You don't even have to touch it, it will find you itself and hit you. Mother needs: effect, listeners, witnesses, and significance. She is gifted with much, but the bouncy ball doesn't see where it is jumping... It jumps, it is visible, it is felt, and that is enough for it, the main thing—is for it to be remembered, that it exists... Not forgotten... Inside, mother also has a piece of the abyss, but it's just that mother, feeling this abyss, runs from it, jumps.
Mother's call:
— Why aren't you calling me? How are you, how is your daughter, how is your husband?.." (here goes about an hour of her monologue about politics, literature, stories from the past)... Why are you silent, don't want to talk to me?
— No, I do, I'm just sad... It hurts... (she doesn't have time to say why it hurts)...
— What nonsense, aren't you ashamed? You are just spoiled... What else do you need? Look how much your husband loves you, just don't tell him that you feel bad, be grateful. The most important thing—is for you to be loved... You have lost your mind completely, rejoice, be happy, go kiss your husband and call me in the evening. And call your brother, his son has a birthday tomorrow.
Mother... Well, how to get it across that it can be different... That it can be completely unimportant whether a wardrobe, a painting, a couch, or even a person standing near you loves you. What matters—is whether I love this person. If I love, I take care, I give them everything they show that they need—everything, and especially freedom. Freedom even from me.

Daughter.
There is one more being, and she looks at her, always looks—with closed eyes, in the future and in the past. A tiny being—a mirror. A mirror of meaning. She together with her approached the abyss, and the abyss of the tiny being turned out to be scarier.
The daughter was born absolutely healthy, perfect—as far as the design can be perfect. Something new was born. And the Universe entrusted her with the care of this new thing, and she now looked with awe at this manifestation of the independent movement of the code of life.

A day later they were discharged from the hospital to go home, and a day after that the Abyss began—the daughter didn't eat, grew darker, turned yellow, and, evaporating, slept. The new perfection was fading away, silently, in the light, on pink lace swaddling clothes. It was... There is no such word in the language, just none.
She looked at this born seed, the seed from time to time opened its eyes and looked at mom with a meaningless gaze of bottomlessness. Here is the body, here are tiny, almost transparent in their thinness fingers, there is no strength in them. The seed doesn't understand that her body is sending her back, into nonexistence. She sleeps and melts... And I look at this, I see this, and I don't know what to do with this. It seems to me, all of humanity should fix its gaze on this impossible mistake, and we must urgently correct it, but... the world was full and content...
The doctors said: "All children are yellow at first, wait, she will eat, go home."
Mother said: "She is sleeping? Rejoice, you are free, go to the store, you have time now..."
The husband didn't even notice that the daughter was disappearing, but anxiety slightly settled somewhere.
And I look at her and pray, whisper to all the forces, to everything invisible to me:
— Let us survive...

Another day of every-minute fading away, and another...
I can't take it—hospital, tests, we made it in time, a few more hours—and it would have been impossible to correct. Now the new life lies in an incubator, plastic tubes are shoved into her living, tiny body: into her nose, mouth, veins... The world is compressed and shattered at the same time. I stand, I look, I am afraid to touch, I am afraid to breathe.
In the corridor sits the "support group"—mother and mother-in-law, looking anxious but authoritative, judgment on their faces. I don't want to go to them.
A nurse comes up to me:
— Is that your mother sitting there?
— Yes. This is my mom.
— Well, how could you, you should listen to parents, especially your mother, or else you almost killed the child...
Inside, it compressed even tighter and collapsed... The jaw of my abyss smirked... It's okay, right now that doesn't matter. Daughter. She is being saved right now. Right now, only her. Mother with her lie, the nurse with her judgment—throw it all overboard, it's just chaff. But it hurts...

The new thing survived, the Universe turned out to be strong. We are home. This tiny being, significantly stronger, sniffles in a sweet sleep, and I look at her, always look. And I beg all the forces invisible to me—let her live for a year, two, three, or even better, five... Please... I beg, and the abyss inside me continues to grow.

Hello, abyss. You are black, and you are everywhere—in every corner of my room and in every smile on the street. You are—the incongruity between how things are and how they should be. An unalignable overlay of two drawings that cannot be merged into one. The new perfection should not die without having begun to live, and people should not be blind to this. But people move, rejoice, celebrate independence days, fry pancakes, take dogs to beauty salons.
"A Feast in the Time of Plague"? Madness? Theirs? Mine?

The abyss pulses, widens, and I look into it. By some law unknown to human logic, this black, infinitely dense emptiness pulls you in. I want to go there. The multicolored carousel of life seems like a decorated children's clockwork toy—so foreign, so empty... But there, in the abyss, something is hidden that demands to be known... I approach the very edge, I look, and I am almost there, but there is no step there... There is no handrail there... I will just fall in there. No. Stop. Turning 180 degrees. The abyss is there, I will return to it, definitely, later. But right now I have a tiny miracle of life, she needs me. You must recognize this carousel as your home right now. Right now you are here.

Now she looks at the bustling, hurrying people, rejoicing at endless meetings and conversations, and doesn't see it as a mistake—she sees a beautiful dance of butterflies over their flower meadow. But she understands: that as long as they don't see a bird in her, they will see a caterpillar in her, which must be shoved into a cocoon by all means so that she becomes a butterfly. But as soon as they see a bird in her, they will never accept her as their own again.
Will the abyss accept...? Will the step hold...?

The daughter is 5 years old. The morning after her birthday. She looks at me with the lightness of a spring ray and says:
— Mama, I am already 5 years old, and I am still living..

— Katya 101.mo
