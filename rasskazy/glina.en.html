Clay. [101.MO]

     She carried memory within herself. Every atom of her was the memory of the Universe. She knew it. She would recount the fragments of herself and see the tire tracks of a truck, a strand of hair fallen from the head of a reading girl, the cigarette butt of a patient coughing up the last cells of his lungs. Within her were the wings of butterflies, the fangs of predators, the underwater gardens of lakes, the frozen cradle of life, the unleashed passion of volcanoes, and stardust. Within her was everything that had lived its time, passed away, leveled out, been forgotten, and become nothing. She was everything and nothing. She was pure potential. She was clay.

Clay knew and perceived herself as EVERYTHING. It was grand and... indefinite. There hops a squirrel; hundreds of years from now, she will enter the body of clay, just as one returns home after a long journey. But right now, she leaps in sinusoids across the green plushness and hopelessly scans the soil for the walnuts she hid. There stands an outdoor table: metal, plastic, wood... all of this, with varying timeframes, will return to the exact same home as the squirrel. But right now, the table awaits the weight of plates and glasses, the warmth of leaning human elbows. He is a fighter against gravity; he is form and function. And there is the roof tile, silent and patient; she shelters everything warm, tender, and alive—with all their clutter and treasures—from the anxiety of the wind, the uncompromising snow, the weeping rain, and the solar intrusiveness. At night, guarding the sleep of the living, she looks into the abyss of the dark heavenly canvas, pierced by the tiny glowing holes of the stars. Human hands manufactured each scale, other hands laid them out, and a third pair will someday tear them down along with the walls of the house, right before she too becomes part of the body of clay.

Our clay has everything except a precise form, precise boundaries, and a purpose.

Oh, what is that dripping? Viscous, round, a first drop, a second, a tenth, a hundredth... drip-drip-drip... and it flowed... Now this is not clay; now through the streams, paths, and earth, it is simply mud that flows, carrying within itself both stardust and millions of lifeprints... Someone's boot stepped into the clay, crushed what seemed to be its already settling indefiniteness, and left, bearing away on itself a fragment of the planet's historical archive, or simply mud... And within the clay, in her indefiniteness, a question echoes: "Am I everything or am I nothing? Am I mud or..." And right then she doesn't have time to finish the thought—someone's hands collect her into a bucket, every single piece. Well, look at that—she has a form now. This huge bucket, its rounded walls prevent her body from flattening across the boundlessness, the bottom keeps her from seeping along with the water into the depths, closer to the planet's core, and with every step she senses the shift in gravity: denser—rarer, left—right. Now the clay has no questions; she listens with her every eternal atom to the slightest changes...

The fluctuations of gravity ceased, the bucket was set down, silence, stillness... And then—something warm, soft, resilient, and inevitable, like love—hands, covered in hundreds of time’s roads, plunge into the body of clay and begin to live there. They dance their confident dance, as if created for this very purpose—compressing, distorting, twisting, mixing an existence that was already mixed up. Clay becomes calm, peaceful, trusting; she clings to the fingers, to the rainbow of papillary patterns, to the tiny hairs, she dances this dance together with them.

What is wrong? The hands are leaving her. Why? For everything is perfect now—the form, the texture, they are together... She was left behind, covered from the sun. She waits... And again, a question, again, the indefiniteness: is this the end, is she to be mud all over again, or is it a beginning? But of what?..

The cover is removed—the freshness of the day. The warmth of the hands strips her of her form; the bucket is no longer with her. She is on the table. And she shrinks, whole worlds are severed from her: here they removed a piece with a Roman warrior, and this one here used to be the tail of a rat from Hamburg, and where did they take that piece of a child’s shoe from the end of the XVII century? Here he is, in those very same hands; he is already firm enough to hold his form on his own, without relying on the walls and bottom of the bucket. And the hands perform incomprehensible actions upon him—another dance? The fingers bend, join, pinch off, smooth down, apply lines, pierce holes, and leave it out for dehydration... Farewell, evaporating moisture; with every fleeting drop, I become firmer and more fragile... I am drying out. I am dry firmness. Now, surely not mud, yet one sudden collision with gravity—and I am dust and shards... I am fragility, I am afraid... Clay, she did not know what lay ahead of her. How are you, clay, how is it now? But there is no more clay; now there is stone, hot, scalding hot, all the endless shoes, algae, and grains of sand melt and become stone. Melting. Fire. FIRING.

The sun was contemplating something in its incredible brightness, effortlessly dropping its rays onto the green grass with the sinusoidally hopping squirrel. The plump fingers of a little boy squeezed a bright, colorful ceramic whistle—a bird. Child’s lips, an inhale, an exhale with eyes squeezed shut from effort, and... a piercing whistle, another attempt, and... a trill. This is the purring and the bells of the melted-together atoms of the Universe's memory. Now the clay knows who she is, now she exists, and she sings. Her voice echoes simultaneously across all times and all spaces.

And beneath the old root of the pine tree lies mud, pure potential. There is the squirrel, the table, the roof tile, the whistle... and she is everything and nothing.
